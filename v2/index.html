About me

I develop perception and localization methods that help autonomous robots operate reliably when the world is changing, observations are incomplete, and satellite navigation is unavailable.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Micro Air Vehicle Laboratory at TU Delft. My current work investigates autonomous drone landing at night on moving ships and wind turbines, in collaboration with the German Aerospace Center (DLR).

Previously, I completed my PhD in Computer Vision and Machine Learning with the Intelligent Vehicles Group at TU Delft, advised by Julian F. P. Kooij. I was also a visiting researcher at Carnegie Mellon University's Robotics Institute with Sebastian Scherer.

My broader interest is in building spatial intelligence: systems that can localize, map, reason about uncertainty, and adapt their representation of the world over time. I enjoy research that connects learning-based methods with real vehicles, datasets, and field experiments.
